### Adversarial read

**Is the problem still real?** Yes, and it survived a merge. Resolved with
`tests/css_cascade.py` down the element path the application actually builds:

**Which rules produce those numbers.** At `21a0e93`:

* `styles.css:856` — `.next-cockpit-recovery span{margin-bottom:5px;color:var(--ink3);font-size:var(--fs-2xs);letter-spacing:.09em}`. Specificity `(0,1,1)`. This is the **caps label** rule — the one that draws `ASSIGNMENT`, `EXECUTION`, `WAITING ON YOU`. It is written against the tag `span`, and the value slots are spans, so it captures them.
* `styles.css:180` — `.next-project-value--known{color:var(--ink2);font-family:var(--mono)}`. Specificity `(0,1,0)`. **Declares no size**, and loses the colour to the label rule as well.

So the absence is not too big; **the value is too small, and it is not only its size that is
wrong.** Because `.next-cockpit-recovery span` wins on size, colour and tracking, the known value
is drawn at label size (11.5px), in the label ink (`--ink3`, the dimmest in the palette — the same
ink as its own absence), with the label's 0.09em caps tracking. The absence's own rule resets the
tracking to 0; the value's does not. The value is being rendered *as a label* in the one cell where
it is the answer.

**Does the described approach still fit?** Partly. The issue offers "give `--known` a declared size
at or above its paired absence, **or** lower `--absent` to what `--known` inherits". The second
half should be struck: `--absent` at 11.5px would put a sans prose string on the label tier, which
is the defect DRC-4602 exists to remove, and DRC-4602's AC-2 will then have to raise it again. The
issue's framing of the guard is right and is kept.

inversion has a *second* route — the value being pulled down by a generic tag rule, rather than the
absence being pushed up — and the rewrite adds that sentence rather than reopening the ruling.

### Approach, and the simplest rejected alternative

**Chosen — one shared size declaration for the pair, family overridden on the absence only.**
Replace `styles.css:754` with:

```css
.next-cockpit-recovery .next-project-value{font:var(--fs-xs)/1.55 var(--mono);letter-spacing:0}
.next-cockpit-recovery .next-project-value--absent{font-family:var(--sans)}
```

Net +1 line, +68 bytes. Measured against `21a0e93`: both WAITING slots move 11.5 -> 12.5 for
`--known`, `--absent` stays 12.5, and **all thirteen other pairs in the census above are byte-for-byte
unmoved**. It also drops the 0.09em caps tracking from the value, which is the rest of the
label-register bleed. The precedent is two rules further down the same block —
`.next-cockpit-recovery .next-project-goal-text{font:var(--fs-xs)/1.55 var(--mono)}` at line 1129
does exactly this shape for the goal slot, which is why the goal pair already resolves 12.5/12.5.

**Rejected — add `.next-cockpit-recovery .next-project-value{font-size:var(--fs-xs)}` and leave
754 alone.** Also measured, also produces 12.5/12.5 on both slots and moves nothing else, and it is
one line shorter. It is rejected because the two branches would still declare their size in two
separate places at equal specificity, resolved by source order. DRC-4602 is queued against this
same stylesheet and its AC-2 requires every sans rule to reach `var(--fs-sentence)` or be justified
in the design doc; `.next-cockpit-recovery .next-project-value--absent` is a 12.5px sans rule and
will be in its set. The moment that rule alone goes to 15px, the pair inverts again by 3.5px and
this issue is re-filed. The chosen form makes one rule the size of both branches, so a later raise
cannot separate them. It cannot deliver the value as a one-liner because the value here is not
"these two numbers agree today" but "these two numbers cannot disagree".

**Also rejected — fix it in the emitter** by giving the known branch its own class in
`next-projects.js`. That is a change to a shared emitter used by fifteen slots to fix two, and it
moves a styling decision out of the stylesheet into the bundle, where the cascade resolver cannot
see it.
