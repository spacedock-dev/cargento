### Adversarial read

**The problem is still real, and it is larger than the issue says.** Measured by me against
`spacedock-ensign/drc-4587` at commit `a251ca4a` — the committed tip, not the worktree, which also
holds uncommitted edits to `styles.css` and `test_next_cockpit.py` from a sibling agent and would
have moved under the audit.

**No count is safe to inherit, including mine.** I ran the audit twice while DRC-4587 was still
committing to its own branch. At `a251ca4a` it returned **38 selectors across 31 rules**; forty
minutes later at `4fb5ee6d` ("fix(web): stop absences outranking the values they replace") it
returned **33 selectors across 27 rules**, with six `strong` selectors resolved out and every line
number below `:1083` shifted. That is a fourth and a fifth distinct figure for this set, after
seventeen, nineteen and fifteen — and it is why the deliverable has to be the audit rather than a
number. Neither of my figures belongs in the shipped issue as a target; both are recorded here as
evidence that the method works and that the count does not hold still.

The three figures already in circulation are all rule-level counts and all three are low, for three
separate reasons the audit surfaced. All three reproduce at both pins:

1. **`font:` shorthand is invisible to a longhand census.** `.next-cockpit-work-derived` (`:895`,
   `font:italic 13px/1.55 var(--sans)`), the three `.next-cockpit-reading-*` /
   `.next-session-departure-reading` selectors (`:947`, 13.5px) and `.pc-trail-result` (`:1179`,
   13px) each declare size, prose line-height *and* sans in one property. A census grepping
   `font-size` sees none of them.
2. **Sans is the default, not a declaration.** `body{font-family:var(--sans)}` at `:43` means a
   rule that declares no family is sans. A census requiring an explicit `var(--sans)` undercounts;
   one treating an undeclared family as unknown undercounts differently.
3. **Composed rules.** 8 of the 33 selectors at the tip (13 of 38 at `a251ca4a`) carry the three
   parts across separate rules. Both cases the DRC-4587 review measured live fall here, at both
   pins, and **no rule-level census can ever see either**: `.next-operation-fact--unknown strong`,
   where one rule sets mono at 12.5px, a second flips the family back to sans and a third supplies
   line-height 1.4 — the 163 `strong` at 12.5px; and `.next-cockpit-recovery small`, which sets
   12.5px and nothing else, inheriting both sans and line-height 1.55 from
   `.next-cockpit-recovery>div` — the 18 `small` at 12.5px.

**The membership test recorded in the design doc is itself the defect.** `docs/design-next-ui.md`
states it as "a rule is on the sentence tier when it sets its text in sans and declares its own
prose line-height". Both composed cases above pass the *intent* of that test and fail its *letter*:
the recovery `small` declares no line-height at all, and the `--unknown strong` declares no family.
Re-running "the audit that produced the seventeen" — the issue's own AC-1 verifier — would
therefore return zero while 181 elements are still at 12.5px on screen. That is the criterion the
dispatch warned about, and it is why AC-1 below is rewritten against the composed audit and paired
with the live drive rather than left as the issue wrote it.

**The exclusion set moves too.** At `a251ca4a` there were **two** textareas —
`.next-cockpit-held-field textarea` (`font:14px/1.55 var(--sans)`) and `.next-cockpit-memos
textarea` — plus `.pc-terminal` and `.pc-semantic-timeline`, which are one rule and two selectors:
four non-prose selectors against the one textarea the issue names. At the tip `.next-cockpit-memos
textarea` has resolved out and there are three. Implementation derives the set; it inherits
neither figure.

**Four candidates cannot be settled offline** (five at `a251ca4a`). At the tip they are
`.next-operations-header p`, `.next-attention-risk-identity h3`, `.next-operation-identity strong`
and `.pc-trail-top strong`; `.next-attention-open strong` and `.next-cockpit-memos
[data-next-cockpit-memo-field]>strong` were in the set at `a251ca4a` and are not at the tip. Each
has a sibling or possible-ancestor rule setting mono, and CSS alone cannot say whether that rule
sits on an ancestor of the target element. Only the rendered DOM answers it. An offline audit that
guesses either way is wrong somewhere: guessing "ancestor" silently drops
`.next-operations-header p` and `.next-attention-risk-identity h3`, which are real 13.5px sans
prose — I reproduced exactly that false negative in a first pass over the same file.

**No product decision is missing.** The register classifier (sans is the board talking, mono is a
string a source published) is settled and this issue applies it. Nothing here reopens it.

**Adjacency, not scope.** [DRC-4596](https://linear.app/recce/issue/DRC-4596/make-the-asset-test-enforce-font-size-not-the-contrast-that-already)
adds the asset-test guardrail that enforces size. The composed audit this issue builds is the
natural engine for that guardrail. Keeping them separate is right — this issue owns the audit and
the raise, DRC-4596 owns wiring it into the asset test — but whoever takes DRC-4596 should consume
this audit rather than write a second one.

## The problem

DRC-4587 raised board sentences to a 15px tier and recorded the test that decides membership: a rule is on the sentence tier when it sets its text in sans and declares its own prose line-height. Sixty-seven rules qualified and all sixty-seven moved.

The same test also catches nineteen further rules that did not move. A rule-level census is not enough on its own, and this is the harder half. DRC-4587's review drove the live board and found sans prose still rendering at 12.5px in places no single-rule test can see: eighteen `small` in the recovery block, and one hundred and sixty-three `strong` under `.next-operation-fact--unknown`. Those are composed across three rules that each carry one part of the condition — one sets the size and a mono family, a second flips the family back to sans, a third supplies the prose line-height — so no census that reads one rule at a time will ever join them. The audit this issue runs must compose the cascade, or be paired with a live drive that reads computed styles.

DRC-4587's AC-1 was written as "every sans text-bearing rule resolves to 15px or larger" while its `Verified by` clause named only the recorded inventory. The inventory was satisfied; the universal clause was not. The criterion was accepted on its verifier and this issue carries the remainder, so the gap is scheduled rather than implied away.

`docs/design-next-ui.md` already records the gap in the design's own words, under "The floor is not yet universal, and this is the gap to close next". That paragraph is the specification for this issue and is removed by it.

## The solution

Read the seventeen one selector at a time and raise the ones that are sentences to `var(--fs-sentence)`.

A sweep is the wrong instrument and would be wrong twice: **two of the seventeen are not sentences at all** — a textarea at `styles.css:871` and the prototype terminal at `:1139` — so a mechanical pass over the classifier's output would restyle input chrome as prose. Each rule is judged, and the ones that are excluded are named with the reason, the same way DRC-4587 named the three tracking groups it kept and the eighteen rules it did not cap at `--measure`.

The rejected alternative is widening DRC-4587 to carry these. It was rejected there and the reason still holds: another seventeen rules of review surface took that change to 142% of a declared estimate, and an estimate exceeded to avoid a follow-up issue is how a narrow change becomes a wide one without anyone deciding to widen it.
