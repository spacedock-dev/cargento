## Why this is in the Clean and Cogent UI/UX milestone

Added to the milestone on 2026-09-17 by the first officer, on the captain's standing rule that a gap deferred out of a pull request is worked inside the same milestone rather than filed and forgotten.

This is the remainder of DRC-4590's control-primitive criterion. That criterion read as universal — no control rule declares its own radius or resting border — and a sweep found twenty-four resting control rules across six corner treatments, which no honest change could close in one pass. It was accepted on an enumerated verifier with the exempt rules named and their reasons recorded, and these five carry the rest. ## A verifier weaker than the claim it stands for

Added 2026-09-17 from DRC-4590's implementation, which found it while resolving all seven collapsed control pairs through the cascade rather than reading the rules.

`.next-action` promises the sentence tier, and **one of the seven rules** DRC-4590 **collapsed onto it overrides that back down**: `.next-session-copy` resolves to 11.5px. DRC-4590's own AC-1 verifier cannot see this, because it checks `border-radius` and `border:1px` only — so the criterion passes while the primitive it establishes is not uniform in the property a reader actually sees.

This is the same family as the three guards that cost DRC-4587 four review cycles: green, and measuring something narrower than the claim. A criterion about a control *recipe* that checks only two of the recipe's properties will keep passing as the others drift.

So this issue carries two things rather than one. The five remaining recipes are the obvious half. The other half is that **the verifier must resolve what a control actually computes** — family, size and the border properties together — rather than grepping two declarations, or the enumerated set will be certified complete while the tier it promises is not held.

Not a regression: `.next-session-copy` reads 11.5px on `main` too, and the pair the implementation checked is two regions rather than the two branches of one slot. It was logged here rather than promoted into the pull request in flight.

## The user value

**The board can be read at a glance: you can tell a label from its answer, a figure from a gap, and the one thing to do on a screen from the prose around it.**

Users report low contrast. Every text ink in the cockpit passes WCAG AA — ink3, the dimmest, is 5.67:1 on panel, nearer AAA than the AA floor. Measured size-aware with APCA, the same inks fail badly at the sizes they are actually set in: at 12.5px/400 even `#f4f1e8`, the brightest ink in the palette, scores Lc 98.0 against a body-text requirement of 100, and the 9.5px and 10px label steps fall off the bottom of the lookup table entirely.

So there is no colour left to spend and no contrast fix available. The work is to raise the sentence tier, collapse the label tier, and give label, value and absence three distinct registers instead of the one dim ink that currently carries 89% of the characters on Held to.

## What is left

Fifteen issues. Three are foundations and everything else depends on them:

* **Raise board sentences to a 15px tier** and collapse six sub-12px tokens into one label tier.
* **Split the three inks onto label, value and absence roles**, so a label stops sharing its ink with its own answer.
* **Always render the Held to reading control**, present and inert with its reason, instead of deleting the tab's only verb exactly when a newcomer looks for it.

Then: the briefing, the tab strip, a control primitive with a primary action on the one tab that has one, caveat tiering, Held to ordering, Console ordering, the scope rail, the timeline filter, and a guardrail so the asset test enforces size rather than the contrast that already passes. Nothing outside this milestone. `type-scale` and `ink-roles` gate most of the rest; `ink-roles` itself waits on `type-scale`.

### Summary

fourteen control selectors declaring a non-zero radius or resting border, decomposing exactly into
the primitive, seven exempt, one text input, and these five. The issue's own headline figure of 24
